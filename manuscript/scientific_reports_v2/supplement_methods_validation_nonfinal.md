# Supplementary Information

## Suppression-aware Bayesian estimation of county mortality using public aggregate constraints: U.S. epilepsy, 2019–2024

**Authors:** {{AUTHOR_BLOCK}}

## Supplementary Methods S1. Public-data reconciliation

The analysis combined county-year, county-period, state-year, and national-year CDC WONDER Multiple Cause of Death extracts for deaths mentioning ICD-10 G40 or G41 during 2019–2024. All inputs were public aggregate tables. The frozen query manifests record database, outcome definition, years, grouping variables, filters, download dates, filenames, and checksums. Exact positive county-year cells and explicit zeros were fixed. Suppressed county-year cells were bounded integers from 1 through 9.

The final model frame contained 18,852 county-year observations for 3,142 counties: 1,350 exact positive cells, 7,807 explicit zeros, and 9,695 suppressed positive cells. The reconciled national total was 58,380 deaths. Counties lacking direct covariate matches were retained so that the mortality margins were not changed by covariate availability. Imputation flags remain in the model frame and are summarized in Supplementary Table S{{COVARIATE_IMPUTATION_TABLE}}.

## Supplementary Methods S2. Constraint matrix, rank, and redundancy

After substituting fixed exact and zero county-year counts, the suppressed cells formed a latent vector

\[
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{9695}.
\]

Each coordinate had lower bound 1 and upper bound 9. County-period exact totals, state-year totals, national-year totals, and the grand total were represented as sparse linear rows. County-period suppressed totals were represented as lower and upper inequalities. The nominal system contained:

| Constraint class | Nominal count | Mathematical role |
|---|---:|---|
| County-period equalities | 1,420 | exact six-year county totals |
| County-period interval inequalities | 1,722 | bounded six-year county totals |
| State-year equalities | 306 | exact state/DC annual totals |
| National-year equalities | 6 | higher-level reconciliation checks |
| Grand-total equality | 1 | higher-level reconciliation check |
| Latent cell bounds | 9,695 lower and 9,695 upper | suppressed positive cell support |

Rows containing no latent coefficients after fixed-count substitution were classified as zero-information rows. The reduced nonzero equality matrix contained 1,259 rows, rank 1,256, and nullity 8,439. Three dependencies were intrinsic to the connected margin system. Seven higher-level rows—the six national-year totals and grand total—were algebraically implied by lower-level totals. They were retained in validation output but were not counted as independent identifying information.

The analysis therefore does not assert that the public tables uniquely identify protected cells. The posterior distribution is defined over a high-dimensional bounded integer fiber, with information supplied jointly by public constraints, the negative-binomial likelihood, covariates, hierarchical effects, and priors.

## Supplementary Methods S3. Feasible initialization

Feasible starting states were generated by mixed-integer linear programming. The objective was randomized while all cell bounds, exact county-period totals, county-period inequalities, and state-year equalities were enforced. Production used eight prespecified initialization seeds (57291–57298). Each resulting state underwent the same independent validator used for retained draws. Pairwise L1 distances, numbers of differing free cells, and category-total differences were recorded before sampling. Production was blocked if starts were not distinct or any initialization failed a constraint.

## Supplementary Methods S4. Joint target density

For latent counts \(\boldsymbol{y}\) and parameters \(\boldsymbol{\theta}\), the constrained target was

\[
\pi(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{\theta})
\propto
\mathbf{1}\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{F}\}
\prod_{i,t}p_{NB2}(y_{it}\mid\mu_{it},\kappa)
\,p(\boldsymbol{\theta}),
\]

where \(\mathcal{F}\) is the public feasible set. For the NB2 parameterization,

\[
p(y\mid\mu,\kappa)
=
\frac{\Gamma(y+\kappa)}{\Gamma(\kappa)\Gamma(y+1)}
\left(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa+\mu}\right)^{\kappa}
\left(\frac{\mu}{\kappa+\mu}\right)^y.
\]

The complete linear predictor and priors are given in the main Methods. State and year Gaussian normalizing terms use dimensions \(S-1\) and \(T-1\), respectively, because the random effects are projected to sum-zero subspaces. This corrected a target-density error in the archived v1.1.1 implementation, which used the unconstrained dimensions and thereby introduced one unintended factor of \(1/\sigma\) for each centered effect family.

## Supplementary Methods S5. Latent-count proposal classes

### State-year transfer

Within a fixed state and year, one unit is transferred from a donor cell to a recipient cell when both updated cells remain inside their bounds and all applicable county-period restrictions remain valid. The proposal records the eligible donor and recipient sets so the Metropolis ratio can include unequal forward and reverse selection probabilities.

### County-period interval exploration

For counties whose six-year total is bounded rather than exact, local proposals change the county-period total while preserving state-year margins through a compensating county. Eligibility is recomputed at the proposed state.

### Interval-path transfer

A path proposal moves count mass across more than one year to traverse interval endpoints that cannot be reached by a single local transfer while preserving geographic annual totals. Forward and reverse path probabilities are included in the acceptance ratio.

### 2×2 swap

For two counties and two years, an alternating \(+1,-1,-1,+1\) update preserves both county-period and state-year margins when all four cells are free and remain within bounds.

### General alternating-cycle swap

Structural zeros can make the county-by-year support graph nonchordal, leaving feasible fibers that 2×2 moves cannot connect. The repaired sampler identifies alternating cycles and applies \(+1,-1,+1,-1,\ldots\) updates around cycles up to the prespecified half-length. This includes 2×2 moves as the four-edge special case but can traverse chordless six-cycles and longer admissible structures.

### Blocked refresh

At prespecified intervals, a bounded subset of related cells is refreshed conditional on the applicable margins. The blocked kernel is used as an additional mixing move, not as a substitute for the cycle-complete proposal family.

Every count proposal is evaluated under the NB2 likelihood contribution for changed cells and the exact proposal ratio. Parameter proposals are evaluated only after the joint log posterior is refreshed for the current latent state.

## Supplementary Methods S6. Exact finite-state validation

Two hand-constructed systems were fully enumerated.

### Chordless six-cycle structural-support case

The feasible set contained two states. The legacy move family produced two strongly connected components. The repaired kernel produced one component, transition-row error \(4.44\times10^{-16}\), stationarity error \(1.67\times10^{-16}\), and detailed-balance error \(1.39\times10^{-17}\). Exact posterior probabilities were 0.5331949 and 0.4668051; empirical probabilities were 0.5353966 and 0.4646034, for maximum absolute error 0.0022017.

### Cross-year interval-endpoint path case

The feasible set again contained two states. The legacy restricted move family produced two components, whereas the repaired kernel produced one. Transition-row error was zero, stationarity error was \(3.33\times10^{-16}\), and detailed-balance error was \(2.50\times10^{-16}\). Exact posterior probabilities were 0.7173542 and 0.2826458; empirical probabilities were 0.7146552 and 0.2853448, for maximum absolute error 0.0026990.

Randomized exact validation generated additional small bounded systems, enumerated their feasible states, and compared exact targets with transition-matrix and long-run empirical behavior. The machine-readable case definitions, transition matrices, diagnostics, and random seeds are archived in `outputs/scientific_reports_v2/exact_kernel_validation` and `outputs/scientific_reports_v2/random_exact_validation`.

## Supplementary Methods S7. Full-data latent exploration

The full feasible system had 9,695 latent variables and equality nullity 8,439 before bounds and county-period inequalities. Practical exploration was therefore evaluated through multiple complementary functions rather than per-cell R-hat for all latent coordinates. Prespecified monitored functions included rurality totals, SVI totals, representative suppressed county-period totals, L1 movement from the initial state, fraction of free cells changed, count-move acceptance, and log-posterior behavior. Deterministic totals implied by hard equalities were labeled as such and excluded from convergence thresholds.

The corrected extended pilot moved a median 59.6% of free cells from the deliberately dispersed starts and passed its prespecified primary-parameter and latent-summary gates. Those pilot estimates were used only for tuning and were not transferred into the manuscript Results.

## Supplementary Methods S9. Truth-known calibration

A deterministic state-clustered panel retained six states, 72 counties, six years, all four rurality categories, and broad population variation. Complete counts were generated under known NB2 parameters. The disclosure transformation then produced exact counts, explicit zeros, and bounded 1–9 cells, after which compatible county-period, state-year, national-year, and grand-total tables were constructed.

The single-replicate tuning experiment established an operational proposal profile. Its tuned extension used four chains, 1,800 diagnostic tail draws per chain, zero validation failures, maximum primary R-hat 1.0220, minimum primary bulk ESS 152.6, and minimum primary tail ESS 290.8. All six prespecified coefficient truths and the suppressed-cell truths were covered in that replicate, but these values were explicitly treated as one-replicate diagnostics rather than coverage estimates.

Calibration batch 1 contains four prespecified scenarios:

| Scenario | Baseline rate per 100,000 | NB2 kappa | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Baseline | 3.4 | 10 | reference simulation |
| Lower event rate | 2.4 | 10 | increased suppression burden |
| Higher event rate | 5.0 | 10 | reduced suppression burden |
| Stronger overdispersion | 3.4 | 4 | dispersion stress test |

Each replicate uses four corrected chains, 24,000 iterations, 6,000 burn-in iterations, thinning of 10, and 1,800 retained draws per chain. Coefficient and latent-count recovery are aggregated with exact binomial uncertainty. Further batches are required before nominal coverage is asserted.

## Supplementary Methods S11. Residual spatial diagnostic

The diagnostic reconstructs posterior-mean expected county-year deaths from the posterior mean parameter vector, aggregates observed posterior-mean and expected counts to county-period totals, and calculates raw and conditional Pearson residuals. Row-standardized 2024 Census county-adjacency weights exclude self-neighbors and counties outside the model universe. Global Moran's I uses 9,999 fixed-seed permutations. Within-state statistics use 999 permutations where at least ten connected counties are available. A global positive Moran statistic ≥0.02 with two-sided permutation P≤0.05, or analogous signals in at least three states, triggers the structured spatial sensitivity.

This diagnostic conditions on posterior means and is not a full posterior distribution of Moran's I. It is used as a transparent trigger and limitation assessment, not as causal evidence.
